# Consistency Bible

This document protects the visual continuity of the cinematic prologue.

## The Mourner

- Gender-ambiguous adult.
- Dark wool funeral coat.
- Mostly shown from behind.
- Face hidden, obscured, cropped, or softly out of focus.
- Emotion shown through posture, stillness, hand tension, and distance.
- No theatrical crying.
- No clear grieving face.
- No heroic posture.
- No glamor styling.

Suggested visual markers:

- Damp wool texture.
- Slightly hunched shoulders.
- Hands near photograph, coat pocket, or side.
- Simple dark shoes.
- No distinctive jewelry or readable accessories.

Reject if:

- The mourner looks like a fashion model.
- The mourner faces camera clearly.
- The scene becomes melodramatic.
- The pose looks heroic, supernatural, or horror-styled.

## The Photograph

- Old printed photo.
- Worn off-white border.
- Slight damage, softened corners, faint stains.
- Pictured face never identifiable.
- Hidden QR area should be blank, engraved, or obscured for later coded reveal.
- Generated artwork must not contain a real QR code.

Suggested details:

- Slight curl to the paper.
- Rain droplet or thumb mark.
- Faded silver-gelatin or old color-print tone.
- Back side may show blank paper texture only, no readable writing.

Reject if:

- It contains readable text.
- It contains a QR code.
- It shows an identifiable face.
- It looks like a modern phone photo or glossy marketing print.

## The Archive Mansion

- Monumental early-20th-century stone estate.
- Restrained Beaux-Arts and gothic influence.
- Copper roof.
- Realistic stone, wet surfaces, fog.
- Distant until the login or claim experience.
- Amber windows and entrance lights are restrained.

It is not:

- A castle.
- A haunted house.
- A fantasy palace.
- A sci-fi base.
- A luxury hotel ad.

Reject if:

- It has turrets, fantasy spires, lightning, bats, gargoyles as horror motifs, or magical portals.
- It looks abandoned, haunted, or threatening.
- It looks too close too early.

## Light Rules

- Cold light dominates the first half.
- Amber-gold light appears only when preservation becomes possible.
- Gold light is practical, archival, and restrained.
- No oversaturated gold.
- No neon.
- No holographic effects.
- No magical beams that feel fantasy.

## Camera Rules

Preferred camera language:

- ARRI Alexa 35 or ARRI Alexa 65.
- Vintage anamorphic lens.
- 32mm, 40mm, 50mm, 65mm, 85mm, 100mm macro, or 135mm.
- Subtle 35mm grain.
- Restrained halation.
- Realistic practical lighting.
- Shallow depth of field where emotionally appropriate.

Avoid:

- Drone-like fantasy flyovers.
- Wide-angle distortion.
- Hyperreal HDR.
- Orange-and-teal blockbuster grading.

## Composition Rules

- Use 16:9 master frames.
- Keep subjects center-safe for mobile.
- Leave negative space for coded text.
- Do not bake text into images.
- Do not place critical details at extreme edges.
- Allow silence and emptiness.

## Palette

Starting palette:

- Charcoal black.
- Rain gray.
- Faded winter blue.
- Dark brown.
- Wet stone.
- Off-white paper.

Emerging palette:

- Antique amber.
- Tarnished gold.
- Warm window light.
- Subtle copper.

Never use:

- Neon cyan.
- Saturated purple.
- Bright orange gold.
- Corporate blue.
- Cheerful app green.

## Continuity Review

Before approving any scene, compare it against:

1. The approved mourner reference.
2. The approved photograph reference.
3. The approved mansion reference.
4. The current color progression.
5. The no-text/no-QR/no-UI rule.

## Approval Dependencies

- Approve the mourner before generating Assets 03, 08, 11, 22, and 25.
- Approve the photograph before generating Assets 05, 06, 13, 14, 15, and 19.
- Approve exactly one mansion before generating Assets 10, 23, 27, and 28.
- Do not mix architecture candidates after approval.
- Do not change the mourner coat, build, shoes, or hairstyle between scenes.
- Do not change the photograph dimensions, border color, corner wear, blank square area, or obscured-face treatment between scenes.

## Production Handoff Rules

- Image generators receive the relevant scene prompt plus the canonical reference image.
- Video-effects artists receive approved stills, the effects guide, and exact matte alignment notes.
- Audio designers receive the audio timeline and separate stem list, not a request for a single final score.
- Frontend developers receive the manifest, final copy, timing table, and reduced-motion requirements.
- Creative directors approve in batches, not scene-by-scene after the entire sequence is generated.
